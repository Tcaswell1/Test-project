History of International Falls

A short look at the town's river trade, early settlement, and border location.

Life in International Falls

International Falls is a small town in northern Minnesota, near the Canada border. The population is around 6,424 people. It is a great place to visit if you enjoy outdoor activities like fishing, boating, and snowmobiling. The area is also known for cold winters, beautiful forests, and nearby Voyageurs National Park.

Early history

The town grew from the logging and river trade that developed in the late 19th century. Early settlers came to the Rainy River area because of timber, water power, and the easy border location between the United States and Canada.

19th century growth

In the late 1800s, the land around International Falls was home to Ojibwe people and fur traders. The first European-American settlement began as a logging camp and river port. The name “Koochiching” was used for the area before the town was renamed International Falls in 1895, highlighting its position on the international border and the nearby Rainy River rapids.

By the 1890s, the region had sawmills, lumber camps, and a post office. Steamships and railroads helped move logs and goods, and settlers built homes, stores, and schools. The town’s early growth was shaped by timber, fishing, and the connection to Canada.

State flower

The Minnesota state flower is the Showy Lady’s-slipper, also called the pink-and-white lady’s-slipper (Cypripedium reginae). It has white petals and a pink slipper-shaped pouch.
